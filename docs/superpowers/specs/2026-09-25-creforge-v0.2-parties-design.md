# creforge v0.2: Joint Borrowers and Guarantors

**Status:** Implemented in v0.2.0
**Date:** 2026-09-25
**Builds on:** [v1 design](2026-09-25-creforge-design.md)

## 1. Why

In v0.1 every account has exactly one borrower. Real bureau data doesn't look like
that. Mortgages are often held jointly by a couple, and personal and auto loans to
thin-file or higher-risk borrowers are often backed by a guarantor (typically a parent
or relative). This matters for three reasons:

- **Contingent liability.** A guarantor's credit report shows debts they may have to
  pay. When a guaranteed loan goes bad, the guarantor's exposure becomes real.
- **Many-to-many relationships.** Pipelines that assume "one account, one person"
  break on real data. Testing that is a core creforge use case.
- **Risk sharing.** Joint and guaranteed loans perform differently from loans to a
  single borrower, and scorecards need to handle that.

## 2. Data model

### New table: `account_party`

One row per person linked to an account.

| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `account_id` | str | FK → account |
| `subject_id` | str | FK → subject |
| `role` | enum | `primary`, `joint`, `guarantor` |
| `start_date` | date | Same as the account's `open_date` in v0.2 (roles don't change over time yet) |

Rules, all checked by `validate`:
- Every account has **exactly one** `primary` row, and its `subject_id` equals
  `account.subject_id`. So v0.1 queries keep working unchanged.
- At most one `joint` and at most one `guarantor` per account in v0.2.
- The same person can't appear twice on one account.
- `joint` only on products that allow it; `guarantor` only on products that allow it.

`account.subject_id` stays as the primary borrower. **No existing column changes**, so
this is an additive schema change.

### Who gets linked

Linked parties are drawn from the same chunk of borrowers, so each chunk stays
self-contained and generation stays parallel and deterministic.

| Role | Products (baseline) | Share of new accounts | How the person is chosen |
|---|---|---|---|
| joint | mortgage 40%, auto 10%, personal 5% | shown | Same region, age within ±8 years (a household) |
| guarantor | personal 12%, auto 8%, mortgage 3% | higher for grades D–E and borrowers under 25 | Age 20–35 years older, grade skewed toward A–B (a parent) |

All shares are profile parameters under a new `parties:` section, with sources
listed like the other parameters.

## 3. Behaviour

Keeping the engine simple matters more than modelling every nuance.

- **Joint accounts.** Performance is driven by the household: the roll multiplier is
  the **geometric mean** of both borrowers' grade multipliers. A strong co-borrower
  pulls the risk down but doesn't erase it.
- **Guaranteed accounts.** The primary's grade drives the roll rates, as today. Once
  the account reaches 90+ DPD, the guarantor can be **called**: each month there is an
  extra chance (profile parameter, default 15%) that the guarantor pays off the
  arrears. This shows up as a cure, and fewer guaranteed loans reach write-off.
- **The guarantor's own accounts are not affected** in v0.2. Carrying stress across
  to them (contagion) is on the roadmap.

## 4. Validation additions

**Integrity:** the `account_party` rules in §2, plus every foreign key resolves.

**Calibration:**
- Joint mortgages have a lower 12-month bad rate than single-borrower mortgages of the
  same primary grade.
- Guaranteed loans have a lower write-off rate than comparable unguaranteed loans.
- Guarantors skew toward better grades than the primaries they back.

## 5. Compatibility

- **Additive schema.** There is one new table, and no column in the existing four
  changes.
- **Different output for the same seed.** Existing seeds give different data in
  v0.2, because the extra behaviour changes account paths. This is allowed before 1.0
  and will be listed in the CHANGELOG under **Changed**. Party selection uses its own
  random stream, so turning parties off (`parties: {enabled: false}`) keeps
  performance identical to v0.2 without parties.
- **Performance budget:** at most a 15% slowdown against v0.1 at 1M borrowers.

## 6. Implementation notes

- Links are applied to **all** accounts of an eligible product, including accounts
  opened before the window, so a snapshot shows mature joint mortgages too.
- Accounts opened before the window draw their starting state from the grade whose
  roll multiplier is closest to the account's blended risk.
- That starting-state table doesn't model guarantor calls, so guaranteed accounts from
  before the window start slightly more delinquent than their own steady state. The
  effect is small and fades within months.
- Measured on 40k subjects (seed 2026): joint accounts' 12-month bad rate is 0.53x that
  of comparable single-borrower accounts, and guaranteed loans' write-off rate is 0.44x.
  Both are standardised by product and primary grade.
- Speed: no measurable change against 0.1.0 in a same-machine comparison.

## 7. Out of scope (later)

- Contagion: a called guarantee raising the guarantor's own risk.
- Supplementary credit-card holders.
- Roles that change over time (a guarantor released, a joint borrower removed).
- Business guarantors and director guarantees (these belong with business subjects).
